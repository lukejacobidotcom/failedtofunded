    How to read this model

    Collections are not accounting revenue. Sales = paid accounts × average checkout after discounts, excluding sales taxes. This is a cash model, not a revenue-recognition or profit calculation.

    The buffer is a balance, not a second expense. Refunds and payouts already paid reduce cash. Only remaining refund exposure and unpaid payouts enter the buffer. Releasing part of the buffer does not create new revenue.

    The refund policy is your proposed protection standard. Reserve actual fees paid on in-progress accounts, not simulated account sizes. Do not assume every fee is legally refundable.

    Runway uses a simple run-rate. Forecast runway = monthly fixed operating costs × 60 / 30. Replace it with actual 60-day fixed commitments in “Cash check today.” It is not a forecast of future variable expenses.

    Growth can consume cash. Unresolved accounts carry forward by cohort. Known-payout percentages are placeholders, not measured payout forecasts. Actual known-payout overrides should include earned eligible amounts owed but not yet requested.

    Capital need is a lower-bound snapshot test. Includes opening fixed-cost runway and the largest projected month-end buffer deficit. It excludes unmodeled daily timing, settlement disruption and obligations. No capital injection is silently assumed.

    Example inputs & safeguards

    The example uses 1,000 / 3,000 / 6,000 paid accounts, $100 checkout, fixed operating costs of $40,000 per month, 4% processing, 50% of sales from affiliates at a 20% average commission, 3% refunds paid, 15% payouts paid, 5% known unpaid payout reserve, and 60 days of fixed-cost runway. Survival assumptions are 50% / 20% / 5%. These are planning assumptions, not actual company performance or approved terms.

    Blended affiliate cost applies to all sales: for example, 50% affiliate-attributed sales × 20% average commission = 10% blended. Payouts paid and unpaid liabilities are different. Add taxes and additional payment/settlement costs explicitly. Refund exposure calculated from cohorts is capped at cumulative sales less refunds paid; an actual override is not capped.
